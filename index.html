The Bicol Region

A land shaped by volcanoes and the sea

Stretching across the southeastern tip of Luzon, Bicol is a tapestry of six provinces bound by a shared language, a deep Catholic devotion, and a cuisine that celebrates the heat of siling labuyo.

Here, the landscape is dominated by Mayon Volcano — the world’s most perfect cone — while the coastlines yield some of the finest seafood in the archipelago. Life moves to the rhythm of festivals, fishing boats, and the slow simmer of coconut milk.

A Brief History

Centuries of resilience and faith

From pre-colonial trading ports to the present day, Bicol’s story is one of endurance against the forces of nature and colonial change.

Pre-1521

Indigenous Roots

The early Bicolanos thrived along river valleys and coasts, trading gold, beeswax, and forest products with neighboring islands and Chinese merchants long before European contact.

1569–1898

Spanish Colonial Era

Franciscan missionaries established towns and churches that still stand today. The region’s devotion to the Virgin of Peñafrancia began in the early 18th century and remains the largest Marian pilgrimage in Asia.

1898–1946

Revolution & Occupation

Bicolanos joined the Philippine Revolution and later endured Japanese occupation. Local guerrilla units played a key role in the liberation of southern Luzon.

Today

Living Heritage

Modern Bicol balances tradition with progress. Its festivals, spicy cuisine, and the eternal silhouette of Mayon continue to define Filipino identity for millions.

Culture

Faith, festivity, and the Bicolano spirit

Religion, ritual, and community life intertwine in a culture that finds beauty in both solemn processions and exuberant street celebrations.

Peñafrancia Festival

Every September, Naga City fills with devotees for the Traslación and fluvial procession of Our Lady of Peñafrancia — Asia’s largest Marian celebration.

Music & Oral Traditions

From the haunting harana to the narrative ballads of the rawitdawit, Bicolano storytelling survives in song, verse, and evening gatherings.

Crafts & Weaving

Abaca fiber, buri palm, and intricate embroidery remain living traditions, producing textiles and baskets that carry both utility and quiet artistry.

Cuisine

Heat, coconut, and the soul of Bicol

Bicolano cooking is defined by the generous use of chili, coconut milk, and fresh seafood — a cuisine that rewards the bold palate.

Bicol Express

The namesake dish — pork belly simmered in coconut milk with a fierce dose of siling labuyo. Creamy, fiery, and unforgettable.

Laing

Dried taro leaves cooked slowly in coconut cream with shrimp or pork and a careful balance of chili.

Tinutungang Manok

Chicken stewed in burnt coconut milk, giving the dish its signature smoky depth and golden hue.

Kinunot

Flaked shark or stingray cooked in coconut milk with malunggay leaves — a coastal delicacy of Camarines.

Pili Nuts

The region’s prized nut, roasted or turned into brittle, candies, and rich ice cream unique to Bicol.
